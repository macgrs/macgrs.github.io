**Course Title :** Survey Methods in Architecture  
**Keywords:** Theory and practice of architectural survey, history of techniques, descriptive geometry, photogrammetry, laser scanning, architectural representation, scientific imaging
**Niveau :** Licence III, Master I [^1]  
**Type :** Lecture (_ex cathedra_)  
**Length :** 2h (+ tutorials/practicals)  

<!-- Résumé -->
The course _Survey Methods in Architecture_ treats surveying as "the first step towards knowing a building". It works through the main methods and techniques: schematic, topometric and photographic recordings, alongside with Saint-Aubin's triad of "Description-Depiction-Representation" (J.-P. St Aubin, 19xx). body of illustrations and case studies has been chosen to show these methods at work and to outline a contemporary culture of architectural surveying. The course sets out the benefits of a mixed survey technique, raising awareness of the interdisciplinarity in the reading of any building, whether of ordinary or heritage character.

## Content
### Course plan

**Part 1. Introduction,** where measured survey sits in the history of the discipline, its qualities and properties, definitions.  
**Part 2. Geometry,** reminders of plane geometry for taking measurements, "Point, Line and Sauerkraut on Plane", then extension into virtual space, "Point, Line, Mesh and Spline on LCD".  
**Part 3. Schematic survey,** the sketches and diagrams that capture a building's morphology or its qualities without measuring it, illustrated by Albert Laprade's sketches and by anthropological diagrams of vernacular dwellings.  
**Part 4. The survey campaign,** organisation on site and production of documents adequate to a commission.  
**Part 5. Topometric survey,** instruments and methods for measuring, establishing a datum. Two surveys, two periods: Carcassonne with the instruments of its century, and the château de la Petite Heuze at Les Grandes-Ventes by Lidar.  
**Part 6. Photographic survey,** retracing the use of photography for the documentation of buildings and territories, from the art image to photogrammetry.  
**Part 7. Documents,** production of the documents, preservation of the field originals, traditional and digital approaches.  

## Prerequisites
+ Descriptive geometry and architectural representation;
+ Architectural design studio (introductory level)

## Learning Outcomes
+ Theoretical elements for drawing up a survey protocol;
+ An overview of the culture of survey in architecture.

## Teaching methods
_Ex cathedra_ lectures.
