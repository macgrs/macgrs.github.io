**Course Title :** Desing of Structures
+ **keywords:** Structural system ; structure principles ; tectonics ; expressivity of form ; construction history ; statics.
**Niveau :** Licence/Bachelor II
**Type :** Ex Cathedrae
**Length :** 8h (+ TD)

<!-- Résumé -->
Whether visible or concealed, massive or slender, a built work's structure shapes the design of the space as much as it sets its volumes. This course proposes a journey through the common structural systems following four modes of redirecting loads and forces: form, vector, section and surface (H. Engels, 19xx); transforming familiar principles of statics into design tools.

## Contents
Architectural drawing is a drawing constrained by materials, by spans, by the ways in which the matter put to work is loaded. From these contingencies one can retrace coherent architectural idioms, aesthetic programmes developed over the centuries, and a wide range of possible forms.

This course addresses questions of structural design and of architectural design jointly: meeting the requirements of a programme (eg. long spans), shaping a volume in keeping with a style (eg. evolution in carpentrywork drawings for baroque churches), or for producing an architectural effect (eg. putting emphasis on structural components apparent heaviness).

Thus, it bridges theoretical knowledge of a built work statics, constructive culture and the tooling for design in the architectural studio.

### Course plan
The typological classification of structural systems proposed by Heino Engels serves as the guiding thread for this cycle of lectures. The division of the course into four chapters corresponds to the four ways of redirecting forces within a structural system. Each chapter opens with a reminder of statics, continue with the description of the type, then have its characteristics illustrated through a case study.

**Ch.1. Form-Active,** the adaptation to forces, working exclusively in tension or in compression. Cables and cable nets through the footbridges of Jürg Conzett; inversion of the funicular curve and the passage to arches and vaults, illustrated by the Catalan vaults of Rafael Guastavino.

**Ch.2. Vector-Active,** the division of forces, working in tension and in compression. From planar systems to space trusses: Polonceau and Ardant trusses in the Belgian public halls, the bridges and timber frames of the Grubenmann brothers in Appenzell, the kites of Graham Bell, where the tetrahedral cell asserts itself as a structural motif.

**Ch.3. Section-Active,** the confinement of forces, working in bending within the structure. Beams, frames and plates: the most widespread system, seen in its symmetries, its repetition and its deformations, illustrated by the development of the shed roof in industry and by the 'origami' roof of the Théâtre de Vidy.

**Ch.4. Surface-Active,** the dispersion of forces, distributed as membrane stresses. From single to double curvature: Schwedler domes and Zeiss observatories, the thin shells of Eladio Dieste and Félix Candela, the current research of the Block Research Group. Running beneath this is a history of theories of calculation, each one widening the repertoire of forms one dares to build.

## Prerequisites
+ Statics / Applied mechanics (introductory level)
+ Introduction to construction
+ Architectural design studio (bachelor level)

## Learning Outcomes
+ **Diffrentiate** the sollicitations modes of a structure ;
+ **Apply** a typological grid of analysis to explain how a building's structure works;
+ Extension of **constructive culture** to every type of structure, from the ordinary frame to shells and tensile membranes;
+ **Recognise** the originality of a work through the structural system it puts to work;
+ **Interpret** the structural approach of a project as a design position.

## Teaching methods
_Ex cathedra_ lectures.

## Assessment
Written examination at the end of the semester.
