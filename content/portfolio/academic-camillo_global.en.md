**A place of spectacle, turning scholars into spectators.**

Giulio Cammillo' (1480-1544) Theater of Memory draws on the shape of an antique theater to reconstruct a miniature of the universe. Mythological references populate its tiers, each representing a fragment of the cosmos. Through the design of this device, Camillo embodies a facet of the Renaissance humanistic tradition, that which sought to embrace the full extents of knowledge through the Kabbalahs and Hermetiscism of his time. 

### _The Idea of the Theater_, assembly instructions
As a preface, *The Idea of The Theater* (Camillo, 1550) opens with a cautionary note to the reader, situating the work in the wake of the esoteric and occult literature within which Camillo’s thought unfolds.

> Following the order of the creation of the world, we shall place on the first levels the more natural things [...] those we can imagine to have been created before all other things by divine decree. Then we shall arrange from level to level those that followed after, in such a way that in the seventh, that is, the last and highest level shall sit all the arts… not by reason of unworthiness, but by reason of chronology, since these were the last to have been found by men. (Camillo, 1550)

_The Idea of the Theater_ contains the description of the _Theater_. It details its mechanism by the means of those fables, how this device allow anyone that takes the stage to embrace the knowledge of the world and present it with the eloquence of Cicero. Despite the distance from a contemporary world flooded with science an techniques, the _Theater_' central themes - encyclopaedic ambition and knowledge consumption - are strikingly relevant today, alongside with the question that has motivated its design: _"how can the world be reduced to a finite set of representations and all knowledge transformed into a spectacle?"_ (B. Scheffer, 1997).

### Crossing perspective between _ars memoriae_ and architecture
Most of the scholarly literature on the _Theatre_ offers an exegesis of it, by the perspective of esoteric traditions or within the development of the _ars memoriae_ (Yates, 1978). This study articulates two research questions, at the intersection of architectural studies and the history of the _art of memory_.

The first concerns the antique theater as an architectural reference for the _Theatre of Memory_. The quality of its filiation with the _ars memoriae_, the art of associating elements of discourse with mental images arranged in architectural spaces ; so that _"the order of places perserves the order of things ; the images recall the things themselves"_ (Simonide de Ceos). It concludes on the mutation of this art, in which Camillo plays a part by settling on the antique theater design as an architectural archetype, withouth however, completing it. The _Theatre_ paves the way for other systems of knowledge, such as those of Giordano Bruno, and distinguishes itself from the nascent rationalism of the 'regular course of ideas' which, from Descartes to Leibniz, would lead to the scientific method.

The second research question establishes the _Theater_ into the history of Architecture. It indicates the posterity of the device, through the personal and intellectual bond between Giulio Camillo and Sebastiano Serlio. An architectural theorist whose treatises would irrigate architectural thoughts and design in Europe, for the next centuries.

Today, the _Theater_ is mostly a curiosity. Whose magical operating system has been absorbed by the passage of centuries and their growing rationalism. It is also a point in space and time, a useful allegory for a mode of thought.
